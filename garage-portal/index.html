Schedule Supplier Standards for Service and Repairs

1. General Standards

1.1. The Supplier shall have a comprehensive Quality Management System in place and operational.

1.2. The Supplier shall have detailed documentation relating to training programmes completed by staff.

1.3. The Supplier shall have internet and email enabled PC systems in operation at Service Reception for booking in a vehicle for service or repair which records full details of the car, the driver, work required, relevant contact details, repair authority and invoicing details.

1.4. The Supplier should commit best endeavours to respond the Network Manager’s Questionnaire as those might be issues from time to time to the primary contact.

1.5. The Supplier grants the Network Manager a non-exclusive and royalty-free right to use the Supplier’s name, logo and contact information for the Network Manager’s own marketing purposes, including awards shortlisting.

1.6. When the Supplier’s Agreement with the Network Manager ends or is terminated, the Supplier shall immediately stop displaying, destroy and dispose of any garage certificate issued to it by the Network Manager and otherwise cease to represent itself as being part of the Network or affiliated with the Network Manager.

1.7. The Supplier will not use the association with the Network Manager or any of its Customers or their corporate identification in any literature or correspondence to promote business without the prior written consent of the Network Manager and the Customer.

1.8. The Supplier shall ensure all information relating to the Network Manager’s business and its Customers, remains Private and Confidential. No such information shall be passed to associated companies, agencies or any third parties.

1.9. The Supplier shall respond to all booking requests received from the Network Manager or any of the Customers within 1 working hour.

2. Customer Service

2.1. The Supplier shall have a documented Customer Service Policy, which is displayed in customer reception areas.

2.2. The Supplier shall ensure all customer reception areas and facilities are well organised, clean, tidy and of a high standard. Adequate customer seating shall be provided in customer service areas. A selection of beverages and a customer area with access to Wi-Fi should be available.

2.3. The Supplier shall if required by the Customer provide free of charge during normal working hours, either a collection and delivery service for customers’ vehicles, or courtesy transport for customers to local strategic locations, such as home, office and station, whichever is required by the Customer. The distance that the supplier is prepared to travel to collect a vehicle will be specified on the Platform.

2.4. The Supplier shall have available sufficient clean, well equipped and maintained loan cars to enable workshop lead times to be met. All loan cars must be less than 3 years old and have covered less than 50,000 miles and have been serviced and maintained in accordance with the manufacturer’s maintenance schedule. Where relevant, the loan car made available to the Driver will come with a full tank of AdBlue and sufficient fuel or charge (in the case of electric vehicles) to cover a minimum range of 50 miles. If for any reason a booked courtesy car is not available, the garage will hire one at its own costs. Loan cars will be provided free of charge to Customers. Terms and Conditions relating to insurance & use of the loan car should be provided to the Customer in writing before commencement of the loan, including, where appropriate, the policy on Congestion Charging.

2.5. The Supplier shall ensure that Customers are made aware of the policy and procedures for “out of hours” collection and return of vehicles.

2.6. The Supplier shall vacuum the interior of all cars (but not vans) and wash the exterior of all Customers’ Vehicles following any service or repair, other than minor or emergency repairs or an MOT with no other work. This service shall be carried out on all occasions and be free of charge (except where the booking is made for a mobile service).

2.7. The Supplier shall provide an express “while you wait” service facility for minor service and repair operations and an emergency repair service for safety related items for Customers.

2.8. The Supplier shall ensure full car care kits are used during all service and repair operations. The kits are to include seat covers, steering wheel covers, floor mats and workshop wing covers. The kits and any key tags should be removed before the vehicle is returned to the Customer.

2.9. The Supplier shall ensure the minimum opening hours for Service Reception are from 0800 to 1800 hours Monday to Friday. The Supplier will notify the Network Manager of any extended opening times e.g. evening or weekend vehicle servicing.

3. Warranties and Insurance

3.1. The Supplier shall give a minimum of 12 months labour and parts warranty for repairs carried out to Customers’ vehicles.

3.2. The Supplier shall maintain full insurance cover for all vehicles delivered by Customers into the care of the Supplier. The Supplier shall indemnify Customers for all loss or damage to their vehicles, caused whilst in the custody or under the control of the Supplier, which is not recoverable under the Supplier’s insurance,

3.3. The Supplier shall be considered responsible for any third-party claims arising from any actions or work carried out by the Supplier. The Supplier shall maintain a minimum of £5m insurance cover to meet any liability arising from a third-party claim and shall supply documented proof of such cover to the Network Manager.

4. Performance of Services

4.1. The Supplier will ensure each vehicle is inspected inside and out on arrival if delivered by the Driver, or on collection of the vehicle by the Supplier (or their agent), which shall include images being recorded to show the condition of the vehicle (inside and out) wherever possible and signed by the inspector and countersigned by the Driver or their nominated agent. All inspection information, particularly any vehicle damage, should be recorded on a Vehicle Appraisal Form and duly signed by the inspector. If applicable, ‘No vehicle damage’ shall be recorded. The Supplier will notify the Customer of any significant damage recorded where the Driver was unable to countersign the Vehicle Appraisal Form. The Supplier will be held responsible for any damage found on return of the vehicle to the Customer, which was not noted on the appraisal form.

4.2. Prior to commencing work on any Customer’s vehicle, the Supplier shall seek approval from the Customer for all the work required via the system. The Supplier shall ensure all additional work required is also authorised by the Customer via the system prior to the work being carried out. Failure to gain authority could result in non-payment for the work.

4.3. Should the Supplier need to employ the services of a sub-contractor to complete any of the work, the Supplier shall be responsible for ensuring the standard of their work is controlled and reflects any standards laid down in this document. The Supplier remains fully liable to the Network Manager and the Customers for the performance of any Services and compliance with this Agreement by its sub-contractors.

4.4. The Supplier shall ensure the correct grade of oil is used in all Customers’ vehicles. Customers will not authorise or pay for a higher grade of oil than that which is recommended by the vehicle manufacturer.

4.5. Where the repair work cannot be completed within the agreed time scale for any reason (including lack of available parts) the Supplier shall report promptly to the Customer, giving the extended time required and the reason for overrun. In such circumstances, a loan vehicle should be made available, free of charge, unless the delay is because of situations beyond the control of the Supplier.

4.6. The Supplier shall ensure vehicle Downtime is minimised by managing repairs effectively and shall ensure the following performance measurements are met:

4.6.1. Eighty percent of all Downtime Management – VOR Incidents are closed within two working days; and

4.6.2. Ninety percent of all Downtime Management – VOR Incidents are closed within five working days.

4.7. The Supplier, including by connected car applications, shall not attempt to contact the Vehicle or any client of any Customer, for the duration of this contract and for a period of 12 months after the contract terminates, unless specifically asked to do so by a Customer, other than to arrange collection and delivery details or inform the client of a delay in returning the vehicle. All other Customer queries must be directed to the Customer’s Driver Support function.

4.8. The Supplier shall not issue service reminders to Drivers of Customers’ vehicles without prior written approval from the Customer.

4.9. The Supplier shall ensure that all warranty work carried out on Customers’ vehicles is fully reported to the Customer’s maintenance control department within 48 hours of the work being completed.

4.10. The Supplier shall ensure that all services are recorded in a vehicle’s service record book on completion, entering the required details and applying the Supplier’s stamp. Where on-line digital service records are in use, these should be updated.

4.11. The Supplier shall re-set the service indicator to the setting required by the Customer. If uncertain the Supplier should contact the Customer to establish the correct setting of the service indicator. If the Supplier is unable to correctly reset the service indicator light they may be liable to reimburse the Customer for any costs incurred because of the Supplier’s failure to correctly re-set a service indicator, including the full cost of a service being performed by the relevant main dealer OEM.

4.12. Workshop lead time for Customers will be as defined on the Platform or otherwise stated in writing by the Supplier and approved by the Network Manager in writing from time to time. These lead times should not exceed five working days if the vehicle is to be dropped off at the garage, seven working days when collection and delivery is required and ten working days when a courtesy car is required. The Supplier shall promptly notify the Network Manager if any lead time changes and shall not be entitled to change its standard lead times without the prior written consent of the Network Manager.

4.13. The Supplier should have the correct tooling and diagnostic equipment to enable it to undertake work on any vehicle that it holds a valid entry for on the Platform. This equipment must be capable of re-setting service/warning lights and be kept updated with the latest version of the software available.

4.14. To achieve the highest franchised or non-franchised status the workshop should have a minimum of three ramps with at least one ramp rated at 3500kg GVW or above and have a minimum of three full time technicians with a recognised relevant industry qualification to level 3 NVQ standard or equivalent.

4.15. Where the Supplier has EV capability they must:

4.15.1. be supported by full time technicians with at least IMI L2 or equivalent qualifications; and

4.15.2. at all times ensure that it has sufficient EV chargers in place to undertake service, maintenance and repair work on such vehicles.

4.16. The Supplier shall ensure genuine manufacturers’ parts or “original spare parts” or “spare parts of matching quality” as defined by the Competition Act 1998 (Motor Vehicle Agreements Block Exemption) Order 2023 are always used for the service and repair of Customers’ vehicles, unless otherwise instructed by the Customer. The Supplier must not charge more than the Manufacturers RRP for any parts supplied, less any agreed discount.

4.17. The Supplier shall adhere to the relevant and current manufacturers’ service schedules, utilising standard service and repair times in accordance with those manufacturer schedules. Where the manufacturer schedules are not available, a recognised and reliable source of data should be used (e.g. ICME, Autodata, Haynes Pro or GT Motives Schedules).

4.18. If, during servicing, the Supplier discovers attention is required to the tyres, exhaust or windscreen the Supplier must immediately notify the Customer’s Fleet Maintenance department via the appropriate Platform for further instruction.

4.19. The Supplier should facilitate and provide all reasonable access to third party maintenance suppliers such as tyre fitters to their location if the Network Manager or the Customer requests it.

4.20. The Supplier shall ensure a forward ordering system for parts is in place to guarantee timely receipt of items to complete booked work.

4.21. If the work on a Customer’s vehicle cannot be completed within the expected working day, the Supplier shall, not later than three pm notify the Customer of the delay via the Platform. The Supplier should use a Delayed Completion Notification or similar on the Platform (as applicable) for this purpose.

4.22. The Supplier shall ensure a system is in place to re-call vehicles for additional work or completion of outstanding work and keep the Customer’s Maintenance Control department informed of such matters.

4.23. The Supplier shall establish and maintain procedures for the retention and storage of non-service items removed from vehicles during repair if this is requested by the Customer. These should be traceable to vehicle job number and available for inspection by the Network Manager. When requested by the Customer, parts should be retained for a minimum period of four weeks under this procedure. If following a reasonable request to retain parts these are not available for inspection within the four-week period, then the Supplier will issue a full credit for the supply and fitment.

4.24. Where the Supplier provides any of the Services via a Mobile Services Unit (“MSU”):

4.24.1. the vehicle will need to be converted appropriately, safe and functional for SMR work

4.24.2. the MSU will need to be able to transport any oil, fluids or other contaminants related to the service extracted from vehicles and dispose of them according to the applicable regulations at the time and any spillages whether on private property or public must be reported to the Network Manager;

4.24.3. the MSU must carry a reasonable amount of tooling to allow for the provision of SMR services;

4.24.4. the MSU drivers must be able to produce evidence of any bookings they are completing if they are challenged (with a view to securing the safety of Drivers); and

4.24.5. the MSU drivers must be made aware that they may be recorded by security systems or subject to specific rules when providing Services in private areas or on a third party’s property.

5. Complaints and Disputes

5.1. In the event of a dispute or disagreement, the Supplier shall immediately contact the Network Manager. If the dispute cannot be settled between the Network Manager and the Supplier, an independent arbitrator will be appointed by the President of the Society of Motor Manufacturers and Traders who will act in accordance with the provisions of the Arbitration Act 1996. The decision of the arbitrator will be final.

5.2. The Supplier shall note in detail all complaints involving customers of the Network Manager, either received directly from the customer or communicated to the Supplier by the Network Manager or one of its Customers. After investigation, the Supplier will submit written reports within three working days to the Network Manager. The report will detail the reasons for the complaint and actions taken to prevent recurrence. Details of any complaints received directly from Customers or any client of a Customer shall be immediately passed to the Network Manager.

5.3. Under no circumstances shall the Supplier place any Customer’s account on stop or refuse to release any Customer’s vehicle without first contacting the Network Manager. The Supplier shall not close a Customer’s trading account unless it has first discussed the matter with the Customer, unless the Customer has not used the Supplier for a period of more than 6 months before the account is closed.

5.4. If any service or repair work is subsequently found to be defective the Customer shall have the right to seek recompense for any remedial work carried out. Alternatively, if the supplier is unable to rectify the default and the Network Manager needs to appoint another supplier to carry` out the rectification work all cost incurred including where appropriate, the cost of any replacement vehicle, will be the Supplier’s responsibility.

6. Records and Inspection

6.1. The Supplier shall maintain records to verify the validity of driving licences held by employees whose duties involve the driving of the Customers’ vehicles. The Supplier will ensure their collection and delivery drivers are of smart appearance and carry appropriate identification when driving Customers’ vehicles.

6.2. The Supplier shall allow the Network Manager or the Network Manager’s authorised representatives or agents to have access to the Supplier’s premises, records and systems at all reasonable times and upon reasonable notice in order to audit and take copies of the Supplier’s books and records related to this Agreement.

6.3. The Supplier shall, without notice, allow the Network Manager or Customer representative access to their premises during normal working hours to inspect a Customer’s vehicle. The Supplier shall provide a senior member of staff for such representative to discuss any issues arising and inspect relevant records. The Supplier shall seek to resolve any issues or concerns arising from the inspection within forty-eight hours of their being raised.

6.4. The Supplier shall make available to Customers on request:-

6.4.1. Historic and current job records, invoices and vehicle appraisal forms relating to that Customer’s vehicles; and

6.4.2. Facilities for approved Engineers of the Network Manager to carry out vehicle inspections on an ‘ad hoc’ basis.

7. Prices, Invoicing and Costs

7.1. The Supplier shall provide invoices for work on Customers’ vehicles, showing as a minimum the following information: date, mileage, registration number, labour hours, labour rate, parts prices with discount, oil quantity used and price per litre, the Customer’s authorisation number. Incomplete or incorrect invoices will be returned to the Supplier unauthorised. For work authorised on the Platform, an electronic invoice should be submitted via the Platform.

7.2. The Supplier shall issue invoices for all Services it has provided in a timely manner and by no later than 60 days after the Services have been completed.

7.3. The Supplier acknowledges that their failure to issue invoices in accordance with paragraph 7.2 above may result in them not being paid for the Services but shall not relieve them of liability to pay any sums owed to the Network Manager for those Transactions.

7.4. The Supplier will not make any charge for labour costs for the fitting of any wiper blades or replacement bulbs if other chargeable work is being undertaken. A labour charge shall be permitted if the time required to replace a bulb or wiper blade, as determined by the vehicle manufacturer’s warranty time allowance, exceeds half (½) an hour.

7.5. The Customer will not accept the cost of additives used during the service or repair of a vehicle, e.g. windscreen washer fluids, oil additives, brake cleaner or any other environmental or sundry charges.

7.6. The Supplier shall ensure ‘recoveries’ derived from warranty claims, goodwill and policy repairs are passed, in full, to the Customer within 5 days of receipt.

7.7. The Supplier shall ensure that they create a “Fleet Assist” tariff in the Tariff Management section of the System and allocate that tariff to all the Network Manager’s Customers.

7.8. Where a Manufacturer Fleet Pricing scheme exists for service and maintenance work, the rate that will apply will be the lower of either the Manufacturer Scheme rate, or the agreed Network Manager rate.

7.9. The Supplier’s standard System Fleet Tariff should never be lower than the Network Manager agreed rate at all times for all Customers, except in cases where a customer’s total spend with the Supplier is greater than the combined spend with the Supplier from Customers of the Network Manager, based on the previous 12 months.

7.10. If at any time the Supplier’s standard System Fleet Tariff is lower than the Network Manager’s agreed rate (other than as permitted under paragraph 7.9 above), the Network Manager will advise the Supplier accordingly and set out a timetable for this to be rectified. Unless the rates are rectified to the Network Manager’s satisfaction within the time given, the terms offered by the Supplier to the Network Manager and its Customers will automatically be reduced to align with the Supplier’s lower System Fleet Tariff.

7.11. The Supplier will not offer any Customer the same or more competitive terms than those agreed with the Network Manager, except in cases where a customer’s total spend with the Supplier is greater than the combined spend with the Supplier from Customers of the Network Manager, based on the previous 12 months, without prior agreement from the Network Manager. If this does occur the superior terms will be adopted by the Network Manager as the standard terms.

8. Use of Atlas

8.1. The Supplier shall update the Network Manager’s Atlas platform with any changes to garage booking lead times, garage capabilities and/or the main contact details for the Supplier or any of their garages as well as any other relevant data the Supplier may reasonably request from time to time. The Supplier shall complete such updates as soon as reasonably practicable and, so far as possible, before implementing any such changes.

8.2. The Supplier shall (and shall procure that their employees, agents, contractors and other personal shall) comply with the terms of use for Atlas, as displayed on Atlas from time to time.

9. Compliance Requirements

9.1. The Supplier has documented evidence that it disposes of waste materials i.e. oils, oil filters, batteries, tyres, etc. using environmentally harmonious methods.

9.2. The Supplier shall enter into and at all times comply with the Data Protection Addendum (as defined in the Supplier Agreement) and has a fully compliant privacy policy/notice in place which is provided to all of its Customers.

9.3. The Supplier conforms with its responsibilities under the Modern Slavery Act 2015 and has published a Transparency Statement under section 7.7 of The Act.

9.4. The Supplier has an anti-bribery policy ensuring adherence to the Bribery Act 2010.

9.5. The Supplier has not contravened any laws or regulations relating to economic, financial, trade, immigration, shipping or other sanctions, export controls or trade embargoes (“Sanctions”) and has in place appropriate policies and procedures to ensure that it does not breach any Sanctions. The Supplier will immediately notify the Network Manager if it becomes aware of or suspects any Sanctions infringements by it or any Customers.
